\section{Introduction and Related Work}
Industrial Digital Twins (IDTs) have progressed towards Stage 4 Autonomous Management systems \cite{grieves2014digital, tao2019digital}, largely driven by Large Language Models (LLMs) orchestrated as autonomous agents \cite{yao2022react}. These Agentic Digital Twins (ADTs) receive high-level requests (e.g., prognosticating turbine Remaining Useful Life) and interact with safety-critical machinery through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) \cite{anthropic2024mcp}. 

Despite their versatility \cite{boiko2023emergent}, ADTs struggle in domain-critical environments \cite{das2025phmforge}. A primary bottleneck is their propensity to hallucinate tool parameters and produce overconfident prognostications \cite{huang2023survey}. In heavy industry, unmonitored agent failures cause catastrophic asset degradation \cite{zhao2023large}. Standard LLMs exhibit severe overconfidence—largely due to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)—which encourages fluency over factual grounding \cite{ouyang2022training, kuleshov2018accurate}.

While calibration techniques like Temperature Scaling and Isotonic Regression excel for single-turn classification \cite{guo2017calibration}, they fail for multi-turn agentic trajectories. Recent approaches attempt to merge verbalized confidence with execution logs \cite{gupta2025calvert}, but overlook token-level log-probability variance. We introduce a trajectory calibration framework leveraging a novel 6-dimensional diagnostic vector, proving its profound economic impact.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Trajectory Diagnostic Feature Vector $f(\tau)$}
Let $\tau = (s_0, t_0, a_0, o_0, \dots, s_T)$ represent the execution trajectory of an ADT on an industrial scenario $S$. To capture cognitive and mechanical friction, we define a 6-dimensional diagnostic vector $f(\tau) \in \mathbb{R}^6$:
\begin{equation}
f(\tau) = \big[ \mathcal{H}_{\text{early}}, \, \nabla\text{Conf}, \, \text{Ratio}_{\text{err}}, \, \text{Var}(\text{LP}), \, \mathcal{S}_{\text{verb}}, \, \text{Ratio}_{\text{loop}} \big]
\end{equation}
Here, \textbf{$\mathcal{H}_{\text{early}}$} measures initial planning entropy; \textbf{$\nabla\text{Conf}$} tracks confidence degradation; \textbf{$\text{Ratio}_{\text{err}}$} is the fraction of failed MCP tool calls \cite{mialon2023augmented}; \textbf{$\text{Var}(\text{LP})$} measures the variance of generation log-probabilities (cognitive dissonance); \textbf{$\mathcal{S}_{\text{verb}}$} is the LLM's self-reported confidence; and \textbf{$\text{Ratio}_{\text{loop}}$} captures hallucinatory repetitive actions. We train Hybrid Trajectory Calibration (HTC) models (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, ElasticNet) over $f(\tau)$ to predict empirical success probability.

\subsection{Expected Operational Cost ($EOC$)}
To quantify economic risk, we formulate the Expected Operational Cost ($EOC$) for continuous monitoring:
\begin{equation}
EOC = C_{\text{insp}} \cdot N_{\text{fa}} + C_{\text{cat}} \cdot N_{\text{uf}} + C_{\text{rev}} \cdot N_{\text{abs}}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{fa}}$ are False Alarms ($C_{\text{insp}} = \$1,000$), $N_{\text{uf}}$ are Unnoticed Failures causing catastrophic downtime ($C_{\text{cat}} = \$50,000$), and $N_{\text{abs}}$ are Selective Abstentions escalating the task to human engineers ($C_{\text{rev}} = \$500$).

\subsection{Calibration and Feature Importance}
Under raw conditions, models exhibit an AUROC of 0.500 (Table~\ref{tab:calibration_results}), indicating complete overconfidence. HTC (ElasticNet) elevates \texttt{llama-4-maverick} to an AUROC of 0.778, while HTC (RF) calibrates \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} to 0.700, drastically reducing ECE (Fig.~\ref{fig:ece_comparison}). 

SHAP feature importance (Fig.~\ref{fig:feature_importance}) reveals that \textbf{Logprob Variance ($30.03\%$)} and \textbf{MCP Error Ratio ($28.70\%$)} overwhelmingly dominate failure prediction. Conversely, the agent's \textbf{Verbalized Confidence ($3.98\%$)} is largely uninformative, empirically proving that post-RLHF stated confidence cannot ensure diagnostic safety.

\subsection{Selective Abstention and EOC Reduction}
Setting a threshold of $\theta \ge 0.75$ allows the ADT to systematically abstain from high-uncertainty trajectories, reaching 100\% selective accuracy with >60\% coverage (Fig.~\ref{fig:risk_coverage}). As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:eoc_cost_reduction}, this intelligent abstention mitigates catastrophic unnoticed failures, reducing the Expected Operational Cost ($EOC$) from $\$12,400$ to just $\$2,100$ per 100 runs—an \textbf{83\% cost reduction}.

\section{Conclusion}
By fusing internal cognitive logprob variance with external MCP tool telemetry, our HTC framework reliably calibrates ADTs, enabling selective abstention. This mechanism eliminates catastrophic failure risks and reduces Expected Operational Costs by 83\%, proving vital for industrial deployment.
